AID — reference

A launchpad where the creator fee is pointed at a registered nonprofit at the moment the coin exists. This page is the long version: what is real, what is arithmetic, and what is not built.

What this is

Open any launchpad and read what it does with the creator fee. Some of it buys back. Some of it goes to a vault. Some goes to the team and is described as going to the ecosystem. The one thing all of them agree on is that the fee belongs to somebody inside the building.

AID is a pump.fun launchpad where the fee is not yours, not ours and not negotiable. You pick a registered nonprofit off a public register, the coin deploys with its creator fee pointed at that organisation's donation route, and there is no function in the pad that points it anywhere else.

Why it can exist at all

There is no charity feature on pump.fun. No checkbox, no partner list, no field on the launch form. We looked, because a pad that claims a platform integration it does not have is the first thing anyone should check.

What pump.fun does have is a creator, a creator fee vault, and — since the February 2026 change — a choice made at launch between creator fees and trader cashback that is permanently locked afterwards. That lock is the whole mechanism. AID always chooses creator fees, never cashback, and the vault's standing instruction is a donation route belonging to someone else.

In one lineThe pairing is not a platform feature we found. It is a standing instruction on a vault that the platform will not let anyone re-point later.

Where the register comes from

The board is not curated. It is the subset of registered nonprofits that have a live crypto-donation route — organisations that have filed, been granted status, and can actually receive a donation in a form they already chose to accept.

That last condition is the filter that matters. A charity we cannot pay is a charity we should not be advertising, so a nonprofit with no working route does not get a row, however good the cause.

Nobody applies. Nobody pays to be listed. Every row carries a tax ID so you can look the organisation up yourself, and links straight to its own donation page so you can give without touching this pad at all.

What "verified" means, exactly

The tick meansThe tick does not mean
A registered nonprofit with a published tax ID That they have heard of AID
A live crypto-donation route we can actually pay That they endorse, approve or partner with anything here
A donation page anyone can open and verify right now That they have agreed to receive money from a memecoin

We do not use any charity's logo, wordmark or branding anywhere on this site. The avatars on the register are generic category glyphs we drew. If a real logo ever appears, it will be because that organisation put it there.

Removal

Immediately, on request, with no form and no argument. The pairing is severed, the row is marked removed with the date, and anything already accrued is donated to them anyway unless they refuse it.

The uncomfortable partThis pad launches coins named after organisations that did not ask for it. That is a real thing to be uncomfortable about, and the only honest answer is that any of them can end it with an email.

The pairing

The launch form has one field that decides anything: the charity. Name, ticker and image come next, the way they do on any pad, but the destination is chosen before a single instruction is signed.

StepWhat happensReversible
1Charity selected from the registerYes — until you sign
2Coin deploys with creator fees on, cashback offNo
3Vault's standing instruction set to that charity's routeNo
4Charity leaves the board as a launch targetOnly by removal

One charity, one coin, forever

A charity can be opened exactly once. The moment somebody opens it, that organisation is off the board as a launch target and the pairing is permanent.

The reason is not sentimental. If a cause could be launched fifty times, the register would fill with fifty versions of it, every one claiming to be the real one, and the whole thing would be a farm inside a week. One pairing makes it a register instead of a race.

Launcher's share of the fee0%Nothing. No split, no referral, no tier.

If launching paid, the register would fill with whoever could launch fastest rather than whoever cared, and the charity would be the marketing rather than the point.

The creator fee

On pump.fun a trade pays a fee, and a slice of that fee accrues to the coin's creator in a vault until somebody claims it. That slice is what funds every donation here. It is not a cut of your purchase and it is not a percentage of market cap — it is a percentage of volume.

Which means the honest version of "this coin supports a charity" is: a coin nobody trades supports nothing at all.

The published curve

The creator's share is not fixed. Under Dynamic Fees V1 it moves with the coin's market cap, and pump.fun publishes the anchors:

Market capCreator sharePhase

1,470 – 98,240 SOL0.95% → 0.05%Tapering along the curve
above 98,240 SOL0.05%Floor
Why the calculator shows a range in the middlepump.fun publishes a curve for that band, not a flat rate. Picking a single number to put in the middle would make the arithmetic look tidier and would be an invention, so the calculator gives the range the taper is bounded by instead.

Claim cadence

Creator fees do not move by themselves. They sit in the vault until a collectCreatorFee instruction pulls them. On a fixed cadence the pad claims what has accrued for each coin and moves it into that coin's donation batch.

  • If a coin traded, there is something to claim and the amount is posted.
  • If it did not, the cycle is recorded as a cycle in which nothing was claimed — a row we publish rather than a row we skip.
  • Batching exists because donation rails have minimums; a claim below the minimum waits for the next cycle and says so.

The donation rail

We do not hold charity money and we do not run a treasury. Each donation goes out through the crypto-donation processor the charity has already registered with — the same route any other donor would use, under their own terms and their own compliance.

The practical effect is that the charity receives it in the form they already chose to receive donations in, and does not need to know what a launchpad is in order to be paid.

The rail is not oursProcessors have minimums, jurisdictions and rules, and can change them without asking us. If a route closes, coins depending on it cannot donate, and that appears on the board as a stopped row with the reason attached.

Receipts

Every donation produces a confirmation from the rail it went through. That confirmation is attached to the coin's row with the date and the amount.

A row with no receipt is a row that says no donation has been made yet, in those words. There is no state on this site where a promise is displayed as if it were a payment, and no place where a pending number is rendered in the same type as a settled one.

What gets posted

ScopePublished
Per coinThe charity, the pairing block, fees claimed, every donation sent, and the receipt for each
Per cycleWhat was claimed, what was donated, and what was not — with the reason
Per charityTotal received through this pad since the pairing, nothing averaged, nothing projected

No leaderboards of generosity, no impact estimates, no "meals funded" arithmetic we invented out of a conversion rate we found somewhere. A list of donations with receipts attached.

Risks, stated plainly

  • A coin is not a donation. You are buying a memecoin. It can go to zero and it probably will.
  • No tax receipt. The donation is made by the pad from fees, not by you from your purchase.
  • A quiet coin pays nothing. No trading means no fee means no donation.
  • A charity may want out. Pairings can be severed at the organisation's request at any time.
  • A rail can close. Processors change their rules and a route can stop working mid-pairing.
  • A promise is not a contract. A site saying the fee goes to charity is not the same as something enforcing it. The receipts table is the only evidence that counts, and it is currently empty.
  • Charities are not a shield. Pointing a fee at a good cause does not make a memecoin a good investment.

What is not built yet

Nothing is deployed. No coin has been opened, no fee has been claimed, and no donation has been sent. The counters on the front page read zero rather than being filled with something plausible, because a dash is not a zero and a zero is not a guess.

The register is real — every organisation on it exists, has a published tax ID, and has a donation page you can open right now. What is not real yet is a single row with a receipt on it, and that is the only number that will ever matter here.
